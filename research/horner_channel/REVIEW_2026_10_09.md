## 3. What she teaches that is testable

### 3a. Candle Range Theory (sweep, range, expansion)
- Range = the 05:00-09:00 ET 4-hour candle (high to low) on index futures,
  gold, crude. Trade on the 5-minute chart.
- Setup: after 09:30, price pushes outside that range (the "sweep") and fails.
  She then bets against the push. Entry choices she offers: an inside candle,
  a "minor high", or a resting order beyond the edge (inconsistent with her own
  "no trigger, no trade").
- Stop just beyond the sweep extreme. Target 1 = midpoint of the 4-hour range;
  target 2 = opposite edge. Day trade only.
- Filters: prefer longs above the previous 4pm close; only when the 5-minute
  chart is ranging (if trending, trade with the trend). Timing: sweeps expected
  near 09:30, 10:00, 10:30.
- Gaps: "ranging vs trending" is undefined; what to do for a sweep above the
  range when price is already above the previous close is not said.
- Internal conflict: the Dow video says never trade chop; CRT says the fade
  works best in chop.

### 3b. Opening-range breakout fixes (ORB video)
- Uses the first 30 minutes (09:30-10:00), not 5 or 15. Also marks 09:30-10:30
  ("initial balance").
- Do not buy the break; wait for a retest into roughly the 38-62% zone of the
  range, first target = range edge.
- Filters: above previous session close, above a 07:00 ET anchored VWAP,
  5-minute uptrend, no scheduled news 09:30-10:30.
- Missing: no stop, no win rate, no trade count. The retest zone is ambiguous.
- Selection warning: waiting for a retest skips the strongest days (price runs
  and never comes back); it also means fewer trades, which slows our
  inactivity / checkpoint clocks.

### 3c. Dow theory (trend definition)
- Trend = higher highs and higher lows (up) or lower of both (down); it stays
  valid until price CLOSES beyond the last swing (a wick does not count).
- Three steps: find the main trend, wait for a pullback that holds, enter on a
  small confirmation with volume expanding. Pullbacks come on falling volume,
  reversals on rising volume.
- Useful as a precise "trend" definition for the filters above. Her claims
  ("trading against the trend cuts your win rate in half") have no source, and
  mapping order blocks / fair-value gaps to Dow's "runaway gaps" is loose.

### 3d. Box Theory (added after the transcript arrived)
- "Box" = a high / low range: yesterday's high and low, the 09:30-10:00 or
  09:30-10:30 range, plus Asian (20:00-24:00 ET), European (02:00-04:00 ET)
  and midday (11:30-13:30 ET) boxes. Mark the 50% line and avoid the middle.
- In chop: sell near the ceiling, buy near the floor, nothing in the middle,
  nothing in the first 10-15 minutes. In a trend: wait for multiple closes
  beyond the edge and a retest of the old edge or the 50% line.
- Alignment filter: longs only above the previous session close (PSC), shorts
  only below it. Claims: a narrow box that breaks usually runs about 2x its
  width; a wide box usually stays in its range; reward : risk is about 1:1 to
  1.5:1; a slow stochastic marks chop.
- Verdict: a discretionary framework, not a rule set. "Near the top",
  "chop vs trend", "multiple closes" and the stochastic settings are not
  defined, and it says to fade the edges in chop but chase the break in a
  trend, which needs a fixed chop test she doesn't give. Shorting near the
  ceiling while above the PSC is not addressed. It adds no new mechanical test
  beyond the PSC filter already in the plan (H4); testing "fade yesterday's
  high / low" would overlap our dropped mean-reversion and return-to-open
  work. The narrow-box claim is close to range-width work we already hold in
  the ORB folder (OR-width stop drafts; not re-read for this review).

### 3e. Volume profile (follow-up video)
- Volume profile shows how much traded at each price. Point of control (POC) =
  busiest price; high-volume nodes (HVN) slow price; low-volume nodes (LVN)
  let price run. She suggests a 40% "value area" instead of the standard 70%
  for intraday (chosen by eye for new traders; no evidence given).
- Uses: take profit at an HVN; let a trade run once an LVN is broken; a
  "retracement" setup (price returns to the prior session's value area after a
  strong day, enter on a trigger, stop outside the zone); a "rollover" setup
  (value forms at the session high, then a reversal trigger).
- Verdict: no new mechanical test. The triggers ("candle break", "Darvas",
  "structure shift", "whatever your entry method is") are left to the trader,
  and the HVN / LVN reads are by eye. The prior-value-area return idea is a
  cousin of our gap and return-to-open work, which found little or lost money.
  Trade-management changes would conflict with our fixed-exit rules.
- Contradictions: says volume profile "does not predict" but that HVNs act
  like gravity; says she does not like reversals at all but teaches the
  rollover reversal and the CRT fade. Claims of "exceptional" R multiples come
  with no figures. A pitch for her community is absent here, but all videos
  push the same framework.
